# What is inside the supplied files

The inspection counted 176 files. It read them without changing them.

There are three business systems, a spreadsheet, two calendars, provider profiles, and a street reference. A business can appear in more than one source, so source counts are not counts of different real businesses.

| Source | What was found |
| --- | --- |
| Jobber | 4,267 jobs and 7,658 visits. |
| Housecall Pro | 4,633 job rows, but only 4,630 unique source jobs. Also 689 estimates. |
| Workiz | 2,657 jobs. |
| Spreadsheet | 206 rows, including eight exact repeats. |
| Calendars | 91 events, with three repeating-event rules. |
| Profiles | 40 business profiles. |
| Street reference | 354 street segments. |

These numbers describe the supplied files. They are not final service-history counts.

## Why the files are hard to combine

The systems use different status names, time formats, and money formats. Some jobs have many visits. Some rows are repeated. A completed estimate is not completed repair work.

The spreadsheet has no reliable job ID or end-time column. Some dates lack a year. Payment does not prove that the work happened.

Calendars include vacations and personal busy time. Some events lack an address but still occupy time. Repeating events must keep their correct local time when the clocks change.

The street file gives approximate positions. It does not give driving routes or travel times.

Some profiles list emergency hours or exclude certain work. Crew size does not prove how many separate jobs can happen at once.

This inspection counted and described the files. It did not prove every answer, find every hidden trap, or measure production capacity.
